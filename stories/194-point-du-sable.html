POINT DU SABLE

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In the 1780s, Jean Baptiste Point du Sable built a trading post and farm on the north bank of the Chicago River, near its mouth

  2. ★

    He is often called Chicago's first permanent non-Indigenous settler

  3. ★

    He lived and traded there with his Potawatomi wife, Kitihawa, also called Catherine

  4. ★

    In 1800 he sold the property to Jean Lalime and left

  5. ★

    A landmark plaque now marks the site of his home, in Pioneer Court, near the Michigan Avenue bridge

Full source list
  • “Chicago in the Middle Ground,” *Encyclopedia of Chicago*. https://www.encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/254.html (archived copy consulted 11 November 2026).
  • Jean Baptiste Point DuSable, an Afro-French fur trader, was trading near present-day Michigan City in the 1770s and was arrested briefly by the British in 1779. “By the late 1780s he began developing a farm near the mouth of the Chicago River, where he also did some trading.” He built a spacious five-room house with a long covered porch. In 1800 he sold his holdings and moved to Spanish Upper Louisiana near present-day St. Charles, Missouri. John Kinzie later occupied the house, on the north side of the river’s mouth.
  • Encyclopedia of Chicago, historical-source note on an 1884 Andreas view of the house (page 410134; consulted via search and secondary citation, 11 November 2026).
  • Describes him as Chicago’s first resident of record, “believed to have been born in Haiti.” Evidence indicates he managed a British estate in Michigan in the early 1780s, “before settling here in the mid-1780s.” In 1800 he sold his holdings; John Kinzie moved into the home in 1804. Also notes that a De Peyster footnote has led many to assume a 1779 Chicago settlement, contrary to other evidence.
  • “Illinois Portrait: Jean-Baptiste Pointe du Sable,” French Heritage Corridor. https://www.frenchheritagecorridor.org/illinois/portrait-jean-baptiste…
  • “A likely native of St. Domingue (today’s Haiti),” but “whatever Pointe du Sable’s birthplace,” he moved in French fur-trade society. Partner and Potawatomi wife Kitihawa, later known as Catherine; Catholic ceremony at Cahokia, 27 October 1788. Settled at Checagou “sometime in the 1780s” on the north side of the Chicago River, not far from its mouth; the land is now the Apple Store and Pioneer Court. In 1800 the property was purchased by Jean LaLime for 6,000 livres; inventory of house, barn, mill, and outbuildings. First non-Native American recognized as inhabiting the land that became Chicago.
  • John F. Swenson, “Jean Baptiste Point de Sable · The Founder of Modern Chicago,” Early Chicago, 1999 (page archived 24 September 2022). https://web.archive.org/web/20220924163339/https://earlychicago.com/es…
  • Farm at the mouth of the river “from about 1784 to 1800.” In 1778–1779 he managed a post at the Rivière du Chemin (Trail Creek), present Michigan City, Indiana, “not at Chicago, as is usually asserted.” Sold the Chicago property in 1800 to Jean Lalime. Rejects a documented family link to a Haitian Des Sables line as “elaborate, undocumented assertions.”
  • “Who was Jean Baptiste Pointe DuSable?,” Field Museum. https://www.fieldmuseum.org/blog/who-was-jean-baptiste-pointe-dusable
  • The City of Chicago officially recognized him as its first permanent non-Indigenous settler. Kitihawa, a Potawatomi woman also known as Catherine. Settlement on the north bank of the Chicago River at its junction with Lake Michigan. First non-Native person to stay and establish a permanent post, though not the first trader to pass through. Sold his Chicago property in May 1800.
  • City of Chicago, “Chicago History.” https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/about/history.html
  • “Chicago’s first permanent non-indigenous resident was a trader named Jean Baptiste Point du Sable.” Home “stood at the mouth of the Chicago River.” (The page’s additional claims of a Haiti birth and a 1770s arrival are not used on screen; see factcheck.)
  • John R. Schmidt, “Who Was Jean Baptiste Pointe DuSable? The Father Of Chicago,” WBEZ, 8 August 2011. https://www.wbez.org/news/2011/08/08/the-father-of-chicago-jean-baptis…
  • Calls him the first non-indigenous resident of the area; notes that much of the early record is fuzzy. In 2006 the City Council officially recognized him as the founder of Chicago. A 2009 bust stands on Michigan Avenue just north of the river, “right near his old front door.”
