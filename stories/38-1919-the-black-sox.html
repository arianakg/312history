1919: THE BLACK SOX

Eight players, one fixed World Series, and bans for life.

◉ South Side (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In the 1919 World Series, the Chicago White Sox played the Cincinnati Reds in a best-of-nine series. Cincinnati won, five games to three, and took its first World Series title.

  2. ★

    Soon, a darker story came out. Eight White Sox players were accused of taking money from gamblers to lose the Series on purpose: Eddie Cicotte, Joe Jackson, Chick Gandil, Swede Risberg, Buck Weaver, Happy Felsch, Lefty Williams, and Fred McMullin.

  3. ★

    The case reached a Chicago courtroom in 1921. The players stood trial — and on August 2, 1921, a jury found them not guilty.

  4. ★

    Baseball's answer came the very next day. Kenesaw Mountain Landis, the game's first commissioner, banned all eight players from professional baseball for life — acquittal or not, he said, players who throw games would never play again.

  5. ★

    The bans changed baseball. Owners had hired Landis in November 1920, after the scandal broke, to restore trust in the game. The eight men never played in the major leagues again, and the 1919 team is still remembered by one name: the Black Sox.

PRIMARY SOURCES & CREDITS

Sources: Society for American Baseball Research (SABR), "1919 World Series: A Recap" and "October 9, 1919: Cincinnati Reds beat the Black Sox" (best-of-nine, Reds won 5–3, first Reds title); SABR, "1921 Black Sox Trial" and "Judge Landis and baseball's ban hammer" (eight players, jury acquittal August 2, 1921, Landis lifetime bans August 3, 1921); SABR Biographical Project, Kenesaw Mountain Landis, and National Baseball Hall of Fame (Landis elected first commissioner November 12, 1920). Told factually, without glorifying cheating. The famous "Say it ain't so, Joe" line is not used: no reliable record shows it was ever said.
