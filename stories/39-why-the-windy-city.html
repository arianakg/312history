WHY 'THE WINDY CITY'?

Wind, talk, or both? The nickname was in print long before the story most people hear.

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The nickname was already in print in 1876. On May 9, 1876, the Cincinnati Enquirer ran the headline "THAT WINDY CITY. Some of the Freaks of the Last Chicago Tornado" — a literal use, about real wind and a tornado in Chicago.

  2. ★

    "Windy" also meant talkative or boastful. The Encyclopedia of Chicago notes that Chicago politicians and boosters became famous for long-winded self-promotion, so "windy" worked as a double meaning: weather, and a lot of talk.

  3. ★

    Many early uses came from a rival city. Cincinnati and Chicago competed in the 1860s and 1870s — over meatpacking and in baseball — and "Windy City" appeared often in Cincinnati sporting news of the 1870s and 1880s as a jab at Chicago.

  4. ★

    A popular story credits New York Sun editor Charles Dana, during the fight to host the 1893 World's Fair. But researchers have found no such Dana editorial, and the nickname was already in use in 1876 — years before that fair contest. The Dana story is not supported by the record as we have it.

  5. ★

    And the weather? Chicago is windy, but it is not America's windiest city. In NOAA 1991–2020 climate normals, Chicago O'Hare averages about 9.7 mph — around 10 mph — while cities such as Amarillo, Texas (about 12.8 mph) average more.
