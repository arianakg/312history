BUGHOUSE SQUARE

For decades, anyone could climb on a box in one small Chicago park and speak to a crowd.

◉ Washington Square (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1842, land developers gave the city a small plot named Washington Square. It is one of Chicago's oldest parks.

  2. ★

    By the 1910s it had a nickname, Bughouse Square, from old slang for a mental hospital.

  3. ★

    From the 1910s to the mid-1960s, soapbox speakers drew crowds on warm evenings. It became the nation's most famous outdoor free-speech spot.

  4. ★

    Famous regulars included anarchist Lucy Parsons and physician Ben Reitman.

  5. ★

    The Newberry Library, across the street, brought the soapboxes back with the Bughouse Square Debates in 1986.
