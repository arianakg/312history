CHICAGO HARBOR LIGHTHOUSE

Since 1893, a white lighthouse has guided ships into Chicago Harbor. It is the city's last one.

◉ Harbor east of Navy Pier (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The lighthouse was first lit on November 9, 1893. Its conical tower is made of cast iron lined with brick.

  2. ★

    It is the only surviving lighthouse in Chicago and one of only two left in Illinois.

  3. ★

    It first stood at another spot on the breakwater. During the 1917 breakwater work it was moved, and its light first shone from the new site on August 1, 1918.

  4. ★

    At the new site it gained a fog-signal building and a boathouse. Today its white tower rises between two red-roofed buildings.

  5. ★

    The light was automated in 1979 and still marks the harbor entrance east of Navy Pier. It became a Chicago Landmark on April 9, 2003.
