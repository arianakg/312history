CHICAGO'S CHRISTMAS TREE

In 1913, about 100,000 people came to see Chicago's first city Christmas tree.

◉ Millennium Park, near Michigan Ave. and Washington St., Chicago (the first tree stood two blocks south, in Grant Park) (map pin approximate)

  6. ★

    Today's tree is a nearly 50-foot spruce from Campton Hills, lit on November 20, 2026

Chicago's first municipal Christmas tree was lit on Christmas Eve, 1913, in Grant Park, north of the Art Institute near Monroe Street and Michigan Avenue. Mayor Carter H. Harrison Jr. pushed a button at 6 p.m., and the Chicago Tribune estimated that 100,000 people watched. The Municipal Christmas Festival Association, led by Art Institute president Charles L. Hutchinson, planned it. The top of the tree was a 35-foot spruce set on tall telephone poles, with a grove of smaller trees around it; the Tribune called the whole tree 85 feet tall. The project cost about $3,000, and the Illinois Central Railroad stopped its trains during the ceremony.

The tree stood in Grant Park from 1913 to 1965. In 1956 it became a 70-foot tree made of many smaller trees, with about 4,400 lights and 2,000 ornaments, at Michigan Avenue and Congress Parkway. In 1966 the tree moved to Civic Center Plaza, now Daley Plaza. In 1982 it stood at State Street and Wacker Drive for one year, and it was back in Daley Plaza in 1983.

By 2008 the city built the tree from 113 smaller trees, and CBS reported that building it cost the city more than $300,000. Since 2009 the city has used one real tree. In 2015 the tree moved to Millennium Park, near Michigan Avenue and Washington Street, about two blocks north of the 1913 site. The city's 113th tree, in 2026, is a nearly 50-foot Norway spruce donated by the Clark family of Campton Hills. It was lit on November 20, 2026, and stays on display through January 10, 2027.

The map pin marks today's tree site in Millennium Park and is approximate. This page shows eras, not every year, because not every year has a photo we can use. The 1956 beat uses a 1958 photograph of the Grant Park tree, the 2006 and 2011 photos show the Daley Plaza tree, and the photo for today's tree was taken in 2017, not 2026.
