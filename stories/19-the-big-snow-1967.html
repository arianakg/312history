THE BIG SNOW, 1967

Two days after a 65°F day, Chicago was buried under 23 inches of snow.

◉ Whole city (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 4 FACTS

  1. ★

    Jan 24, 1967: a record 65°F, with thunderstorms that night. Then a cold front arrived.

  2. ★

    Snow began at 5:02 a.m. on Thursday, Jan 26, and fell for about 29 hours. Winds off the lake gusted over 50 mph, with drifts of 4 to 6 feet.

  3. ★

    23 inches in all: still Chicago's biggest snowstorm on record.

  4. ★

    Thousands of cars and buses were stranded, O'Hare closed, and helicopters flew food and medicine in. Sadly, about 60 people in the Chicago area died because of the storm.

Myth or fact? "A snowstorm once cost a Chicago mayor his job." Fact. After a slow cleanup from the 1979 blizzard, Mayor Michael Bilandic lost the Democratic primary to Jane Byrne, and many people blamed the snow.
