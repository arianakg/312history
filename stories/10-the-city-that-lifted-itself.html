THE CITY THAT LIFTED ITSELF

In the 1850s–60s Chicago raised its streets, then jacked whole buildings up to meet them.

◉ Loop, Lake St (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 4 FACTS

  1. ★

    Flat, low land on clay soil left the streets muddy and poorly drained.

  2. ★

    Ellis Chesbrough's 1856 sewer plan raised streets about 4–5 ft, up to 8 ft in places.

  3. ★

    In 1861 the six-story Tremont House was raised about 6 ft while guests stayed inside.

  4. ★

    In 1860 a row of Lake Street buildings rose 4 ft 8 in in five days, using 600 men and 6,000 jackscrews. Shops stayed open.

Myth or fact? "George Pullman lifted Chicago by himself." Partly. Pullman made his name raising buildings, including the Tremont House, but many contractors and thousands of workers did the job.
