THE SECOND CITY

Opened on December 16, 1959, a small Chicago cabaret turned improvisation into a comedy revolution.

◉ Old Town (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The Second City opened on December 16, 1959, at 1842 North Wells Street in Chicago. It was co-founded by Paul Sills, Howard Alk and Bernie Sahlins, who had worked in earlier Chicago companies such as the Playwrights Theatre Club and the Compass Players.

  2. ★

    The name was a joke at Chicago’s expense. It came from A. J. Liebling’s derisive “Second City” articles about Chicago in The New Yorker in January 1952, later collected as the book Chicago: The Second City in 1952.

  3. ★

    The method came from theater games. Paul Sills, the first director, was the son of Viola Spolin, who created the improvisational games. Sills used those games to build scenes, and Spolin later ran workshops for the Second City cast.

  4. ★

    In 1967 the company moved to a new Chicago home at 1616 North Wells Street, in Old Town. That Wells Street home is still the Chicago home of The Second City.

  5. ★

    The idea spread, then trained generations. A permanent Toronto base followed in 1973. Alumni lists include Tina Fey and Stephen Colbert — two names from a long line of performers who learned the craft on Second City stages.
