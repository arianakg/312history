MARINA CITY

Two concrete corncobs over the river — a city within a city, built to bring Chicagoans back downtown.

◉ River at State St (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    Marina City was designed by Bertrand Goldberg Associates to draw middle-class residents back downtown. Construction officially began with a groundbreaking on November 22, 1960, on the north bank of the Chicago River.

  2. ★

    The towers rose about one floor per day. First tenants moved into the east tower in October 1962, and both towers opened fully in early 1963 — then the tallest reinforced-concrete structures and tallest apartment buildings in the world.

  3. ★

    The rest of the complex followed the towers. Office tenants occupied Marina City’s office building in late 1964, and the theater was finished in 1967 — the year the whole complex was completed.

  4. ★

    Each 65-story tower stacks a 19-story parking ramp below apartments, with laundry and services between them. Semicircular balconies radiate like sunflower petals — Goldberg’s image, though Chicagoans nicknamed them corncobs. Offices, shops, a theater and a marina made it a city within a city.

  5. ★

    Chicago protected the icon on February 10, 2016, when the City Council designated Marina City a Chicago Landmark District. More than sixty years after groundbreaking, the corncobs still anchor the riverfront.
