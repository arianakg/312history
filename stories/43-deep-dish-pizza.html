DEEP-DISH PIZZA

Born in 1943, eaten with a knife and fork: Chicago's most argued-about pizza.

◉ River North (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    Chicago-style deep-dish first appeared in fall 1943, at the restaurant that became Pizzeria Uno. The Chicago History Museum dates the new style to that opening.

  2. ★

    It is not a thicker thin pizza. Deep-dish is baked in a deep pan, with a thick crust, generous cheese and toppings, and chunky tomato sauce on top — so the cheese does not burn during the long bake.

  3. ★

    Who invented it? No one person is proven. Ric Riccardo and Ike Sewell were behind the restaurant, and Lou Malnati worked there — later family and company stories credit different men, and historians have not settled it.

  4. ★

    The bake is slow. The History Museum describes very hot ovens, around 600 degrees, and fifty to sixty minutes for a medium pie — one reason deep-dish is ordered ahead and eaten with a knife and fork.

  5. ★

    Lou Malnati went his own way in time. On March 17, 1971, Lou and Jean Malnati opened the first Lou Malnati’s Pizzeria, in Lincolnwood, just outside Chicago.
