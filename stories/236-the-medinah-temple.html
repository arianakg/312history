THE MEDINAH TEMPLE

Did you know downtown Chicago has a temple with giant domes?

◉ The Medinah Temple, 600 N. Wabash Avenue, Chicago (Near North Side)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 6 FACTS

  1. ★

    The cornerstone was laid at midnight on Halloween, 1911

  2. ★

    The Medinah Temple was built in 1912 at 600 N. Wabash Avenue for Chicago's Shriners

  3. ★

    Architects Huehl & Schmid designed it

  4. ★

    The auditorium had 4,200 seats

  5. ★

    The Chicago Symphony later recorded more than 100 performances here

  6. ★

    The building still stands today

Full source list
  • City of Chicago Landmarks, Medinah Temple (landmark lanId 1367; designated June 27, 2001). Architects: Huehl & Schmid. Year built: 1912. Address: 600 N. Wabash Avenue. The official page describes a 4,200-seat auditorium and the building as one of the nation’s finest examples of Middle Eastern-style architecture. https://webapps1.chicago.gov/landmarksweb/web/landmarkdetails.htm?lanId=1367
  • Commission on Chicago Landmarks, Medinah Temple designation report, May 2001 (landmark L-145). Records a building permit on October 7, 1911; a cornerstone laid at midnight on Halloween, 1911, before about 5,000 members; dedication a year later, in 1912; a 4,200-seat auditorium; and, from 1967, more than 100 Chicago Symphony Orchestra recording sessions at the temple. https://www.chicagolandmarkreports.org/landmark/medinah-temple-l145/ and https://reports.chicagolandmarkreports.org/medinah-temple-l145.pdf
  • Chicago Symphony Orchestra, “Herseth @ 100: For the Record,” July 20, 2021. The orchestra’s own list of recordings places many sessions at Medinah Temple, including work for Angel, London, Deutsche Grammophon, and EMI. https://chicagosymphony.org/inside-the-music/article/4283/herseth-100-for-the-record
  • The Architectural Record 33, no. 4 (April 1913). Contemporary description and photographs of the new Medinah Temple, including the 4,200-seat auditorium.
  • Percy H. Sloan photographs of Medinah Hall / Medinah Temple, early 20th century, Newberry Library (Midwest MS Sloan). Public domain. Wikimedia Commons (NBY 561)
