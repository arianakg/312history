INVENTED IN CHICAGO

Did you know these 12 things were invented in Chicago?

★ 12 FACTS

  1. ★

    Balloon-frame house, 1833. Chicago builders made a light wood frame still used today.

  2. ★

    The skyscraper, 1885. William Le Baron Jenney's Home Insurance Building is often called the first.

  3. ★

    Softball, 1887. George Hancock started the game at the Farragut Boat Club.

  4. ★

    The Ferris wheel, 1893. George Ferris built it for Chicago's World's Fair.

  5. ★

    An early zipper, 1893. Whitcomb Judson showed his "clasp locker" at the fair.

  6. ★

    The Radio Flyer, 1917. Antonio Pasin made the little red wagon in Chicago.

  7. ★

    The car radio, 1930. Galvin Manufacturing, later Motorola, made one of the first popular car radios.

  8. ★

    The blood bank, 1937. Dr. Bernard Fantus opened the first U.S. hospital blood bank at Cook County Hospital.

  9. ★

    Deep-dish pizza, 1943. It first appeared at the restaurant that became Pizzeria Uno.

  10. ★

    The pinball flipper, 1947. Harry Mabs at Gottlieb added electric flippers.

  11. ★

    The TV remote, 1950. Zenith made the first TV remotes in Chicago.

  12. ★

    The Weber kettle, 1952. George Stephen built it at a metal shop on Jefferson Street.

PRIMARY SOURCES & CREDITS

Images: original 312 History illustrations. Cover engraving based on a public-domain 1893 photograph of the Ferris Wheel. Music: original bass.

Full source list
  • Encyclopedia of Chicago, "Balloon Frame Construction," "Home Insurance Building," "Softball," and "Ferris Wheel."
  • Chicago Architecture Center; Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Home Insurance Building" and "Ferris wheel." The Home Insurance Building (William Le Baron Jenney, 1884–85) is often called the first skyscraper; some historians debate the label.
  • USA Softball official history; Chicago History Museum. George Hancock started indoor baseball at the Farragut Boat Club, Thanksgiving 1887, later called softball.
  • Chicago History Museum / World's Columbian Exposition accounts. George W. G. Ferris Jr. designed the wheel for Chicago's 1893 World's Fair; Ferris was an engineer from Pittsburgh.
  • Smithsonian National Museum of American History, zipper history; USPTO. Whitcomb L. Judson of Chicago patented a clasp locker and showed it at the 1893 fair. The modern zipper was redesigned by Gideon Sundback in 1913, not in Chicago.
  • Radio Flyer official company history; Made-in-Chicago Museum. Antonio Pasin began making wagons in Chicago in 1917 (Liberty Coasters, later Radio Flyer).
  • Motorola / Galvin Manufacturing official history; 312 History story 214. The 1930 Motorola was one of the first popular car radios, not the first car radio.
  • Cook County Health / UI Health; JAMA historical accounts; 312 History story 32. Dr. Bernard Fantus opened the first U.S. hospital blood bank at Cook County Hospital on March 15, 1937, and coined the term. Earlier stored-blood work existed (for example in World War I).
  • Chicago Tribune historical reporting; 312 History story 43. Deep-dish pizza first appeared in 1943 at the restaurant that became Pizzeria Uno. No single inventor is proven.
  • Internet Pinball Machine Database, Gottlieb Humpty Dumpty, IPD No. 1254; The Strong National Museum of Play; 312 History story 217. Harry Mabs at D. Gottlieb & Co. added electric flippers in 1947. Not a claim that pinball itself was invented in Chicago.
  • Zenith official history; Smithsonian; 312 History story 212. Lazy Bones wired remote, 1950; Eugene Polley's Flash-Matic, 1955, the first wireless TV remote.
  • Weber official company history; Made-in-Chicago Museum, Weber Grill, 108 N. Jefferson Street, West Loop. George Stephen built the kettle grill there in 1952.
