THE FIRST TV DEBATE

Did you know the first televised presidential debate was in Chicago?

◉ CBS Studio 1, 630 N. McClurg Court, Chicago (Streeterville) (map pin approximate)

    Did you know the first televised presidential debate was in Chicago?

  2. ★

    On September 26, 1960, Kennedy and Nixon debated in Chicago

On September 26, 1960, Senator John F. Kennedy and Vice President Richard Nixon met for the first general-election presidential debate ever shown on television. The debate was broadcast from WBBM-TV’s CBS Studio 1 at 630 N. McClurg Court, in Streeterville on the Near North Side. It ran from 8:30 to 9:30 p.m. in Chicago (9:30 to 10:30 p.m. Eastern).

The Commission on Presidential Debates puts the official audience at 66.4 million people. It was the first of four debates that year. The later debates were held on October 7 in Washington, October 13 (by split-screen, with the candidates in different cities), and October 21 in New York.

The studio building had opened in 1956 as the CBS Chicago broadcast center, in the former Chicago Arena / Riding Club of 1924. It was demolished in early 2009. The Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, formerly the Rehabilitation Institute of Chicago, opened in March 2017 at 355 E. Erie Street, on the same block. The studio itself does not still stand.

The map pin is the historic studio address, 630 N. McClurg Court, and is approximate. The photograph on this page is an Associated Press wirephoto taken in Chicago on September 26, 1960. The printed caption says Kennedy and Nixon are posing after the debate, not during it.
