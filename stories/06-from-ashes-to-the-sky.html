1872

FROM ASHES TO THE SKY

After the 1871 fire, Chicago didn't just rebuild. It built up. Lumber for new buildings arrived the day the last flames died, and in 1872 the city drew "fire limits" downtown where new buildings had to be brick or stone. Architects poured in, and what became the Chicago School of architecture was born.

★ 2 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1885, at LaSalle & Adams, William Le Baron Jenney's Home Insurance Building opened: 10 stories, about 138 feet, on a fireproofed iron-and-steel frame that carried much of the building's weight. Two more floors were added in 1891. It's often called the world's first skyscraper, though historians still debate that title. It was torn down in 1931.

  2. ★

    By 1893, just 22 years after the fire, Chicago hosted the World's Columbian Exposition, with 27.5 million admissions.
