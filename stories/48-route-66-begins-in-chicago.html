ROUTE 66 BEGINS IN CHICAGO

In 1926 a new national highway started in downtown Chicago and ran all the way to the Pacific.

◉ Adams St & Michigan Ave (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    US Highway 66 was established on November 11, 1926, as part of the new numbered U.S. highway system. Signs went up along the road the following year.

  2. ★

    It ran from Chicago to the Los Angeles area, ending near Santa Monica — about 2,400 miles through eight states: Illinois, Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico, Arizona and California.

  3. ★

    Its original eastern end was downtown, at Jackson Boulevard and Michigan Avenue, beside the Art Institute and Grant Park.

  4. ★

    In the late 1930s the end moved a few blocks east, to Jackson and Lake Shore Drive by Lake Michigan. After Jackson became one-way in the 1950s, westbound 66 used Adams Street — where today's "Begin Historic Route 66" sign stands. Historians note that sign marks the tradition, not the official start.

  5. ★

    Interstates slowly replaced the old road. On June 27, 1985, US 66 was officially removed from the U.S. highway system. Today brown "Historic Route 66" signs guide travelers along surviving stretches, from Chicago across Illinois.
