1862

IDA B. WELLS

She used a pen to fight injustice, and Chicago became her home base.

◉ Bronzeville (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 4 FACTS

  1. ★

    Born enslaved in Holly Springs, Mississippi, in 1862, she became a teacher, then a journalist and newspaper owner in Memphis.

  2. ★

    In 1892, after three of her friends were murdered by a mob, she investigated lynching and published the facts in "Southern Horrors."

  3. ★

    She moved to Chicago in 1893 and married lawyer Ferdinand Barnett in 1895.

  4. ★

    She helped found the NAACP in 1909 and started the Alpha Suffrage Club in 1913 to win the vote for Black women. In 2020 she received a special Pulitzer Prize citation.

Myth or fact? "She refused to give up her train seat 71 years before Rosa Parks." Fact. In 1884 she was dragged off a train for refusing to leave the ladies' car. She sued the railroad and won, but a higher court overturned the decision.
