THE CHICAGO PORTAGE

A muddy shortcut in suburban Lyons helped make Chicago.

◉ Chicago Portage National Historic Site, Lyons (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    Native Americans used this route long before Marquette and Jolliet crossed it in 1673

  2. ★

    A swampy marsh called Mud Lake linked the Des Plaines and Chicago rivers

  3. ★

    Jolliet wrote that a short canal here could link the Great Lakes to the Mississippi

  4. ★

    The Illinois and Michigan Canal opened that link in 1848

  5. ★

    The Lyons site was named a National Historic Site in 1952

  • Mud Lake
  • FPDCC (same URL) — "a narrow seven-and-a-half-mile-long swamp, later named Mud Lake. Native Americans used the route to travel between the Des Plaines River and the South Branch of the Chicago River"
  • Friends of the Chicago Portage (CMU Libraries) http://drupal.library.cmu.edu/chicago/node/65 — "The Chicago Portage was the connection between the Chicago and Des Plaines Rivers. A long narrow marsh called "Mud Lake" lay between the two rivers."
  • Jolliet's canal idea
  • Encyclopedia of Chicago https://www.encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/1437.html — Jolliet "writing to another Jesuit priest: '...A canal would need to be cut across in only half a league of a prairie in order to enter from the lake in the River St. Louis (Illinois River) which discharges into the Mississippi.'"
  • FPDCC — "Joliet believed that digging a canal along the route could create a more permanent and efficient connection between the Great Lakes and the Gulf of Mexico."
  • Friends http://drupal.library.cmu.edu/chicago/node/131 — "Jolliet suggested then that a short canal cut through the portage area would create a vital transportation and trade connection."
  • I&M Canal 1848
  • NPS — "Along with the opening of the Illinois and Michigan Canal in 1848, the portage became the transportation link that spurred the growth of the City of Chicago."
  • Friends node/131 — "the 97 mile long Illinois & Michigan Canal (I&M Canal) was completed in 1848 ... ultimately completed the connection envisioned by Jolliet."
  • National Historic Site 1952
