1803

FORT DEARBORN

Built in 1803, attacked in 1812, rebuilt in 1816: the fort at the river's mouth.

◉ River mouth / Michigan Ave (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1803 Captain John Whistler arrived to build a fort at the mouth of the Chicago River. It was named Fort Dearborn, for Henry Dearborn, President Jefferson’s secretary of war.

  2. ★

    The land had come to the United States in the 1795 Treaty of Greenville. The fort stood on the south side of the river, across from DuSable’s old home on the north side.

  3. ★

    In the War of 1812, the garrison was ordered to evacuate. On August 15, 1812, Potawatomi warriors attacked the column along the lakefront. Dozens of soldiers and civilians, and Native warriors too, were killed. The fort was burned afterward.

  4. ★

    The army returned and rebuilt Fort Dearborn in 1816, on the same river site. Soldiers and traders came back, and settlement grew again around the fort.

  5. ★

    Chicago still carries the fort on its flag. The star for Fort Dearborn was added on December 21, 1939 — it is the first star, nearest the pole. That is the next post’s story.
