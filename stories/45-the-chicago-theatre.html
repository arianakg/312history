THE CHICAGO THEATRE

Opened in 1921 as a movie palace, its C-H-I-C-A-G-O sign still lights State Street.

◉ State St (map pin approximate)

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The Chicago Theatre opened on October 26, 1921, as the Balaban and Katz Chicago Theatre. It was promoted as “The Wonder Theatre of the World,” and its first event was the silent film “The Sign on the Door,” with Norma Talmadge, a 50-piece orchestra and the Wurlitzer organ.

  2. ★

    Architects C. W. and George L. Rapp — Rapp and Rapp — designed it in French Baroque style. The off-white glazed terra cotta front carries a miniature Arc de Triomphe above the marquee, and the lobby was modeled on the Royal Chapel at Versailles.

  3. ★

    Its vertical sign spells C-H-I-C-A-G-O in letters about six feet tall and rises about six stories. The sign dates to 1921, but it is not untouched: early on its letters were replaced with porcelain-enamel sheet metal, and its lighting and colors changed over time.

  4. ★

    Protection came in two steps. The theatre was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on June 6, 1979, and designated a Chicago Landmark on January 28, 1983. The City calls it the oldest surviving movie palace in Chicago.

  5. ★

    After it closed in September 1985, a nine-month restoration brought it back. It reopened on September 10, 1986, with a gala performance by Frank Sinatra — and State Street had its emblem lit again.
