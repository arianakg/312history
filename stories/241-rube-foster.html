RUBE FOSTER

Did you know a Chicago pitcher built a baseball league of his own?

Andrew “Rube” Foster was born on September 17, 1879, in Calvert, Texas. He became player-manager of Chicago's Leland Giants in 1907, and in 1911 he formed the Chicago American Giants in a partnership with John Schorling, who had leased the old White Sox grounds on the South Side. The park, at 39th Street between Princeton and Wentworth Avenues, was known as Schorling Park.

On February 13, 1920, Foster led the founding of the Negro National League, the first stable Black professional baseball league. The organizing meeting was held in Kansas City. Foster served as president and treasurer. His Chicago American Giants won the first three pennants, in 1920, 1921, and 1922. The Encyclopedia of Chicago also records later pennants in 1926 and 1927.

The National Baseball Hall of Fame, which inducted Foster in 1981, calls him the “father of black baseball.” He died on December 9, 1930, in Kankakee, Illinois, and is buried at Lincoln Cemetery in Blue Island.

Schorling Park does not still stand; the site is now Wentworth Gardens. The map pin is the park's historic location at 39th Street and Princeton Avenue, and is approximate.
