THE CHICAGO FLAG

Two blue stripes, four red stars — and every star was added for a reason.

◉ City Hall (map pin approximate)

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    Chicago adopted its flag on April 4, 1917. Wallace Rice, a writer and lecturer on flag design, won the city’s design competition — more than 1,000 entries were submitted.

  2. ★

    The first flag had only two red six-pointed stars: one for the Great Chicago Fire of October 8–10, 1871, and one for the World’s Columbian Exposition of 1893.

  3. ★

    A third star was added on October 9, 1933, for the Century of Progress exposition. A fourth was added on December 21, 1939, for Fort Dearborn — the fort in our last post.

  4. ★

    The two blue stripes are water. The top stripe stands for Lake Michigan and the North Branch of the river; the bottom stripe, for the South Branch and the Great Canal. The white stripes stand for the North, West and South sides.

  5. ★

    People often propose a fifth star, for floods, sports, music and more. But the official flag in Chicago’s Municipal Code still has four stars — no fifth star has been adopted.
