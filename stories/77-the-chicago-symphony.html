THE CHICAGO SYMPHONY

A famous conductor said he would go anywhere for a permanent orchestra. Chicago gave him one.

◉ Orchestra Hall, Michigan Ave (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1889, Chicago businessman Charles Norman Fay asked conductor Theodore Thomas if he would come to Chicago for a permanent orchestra. Thomas reportedly answered: "I would go to hell if they gave me a permanent orchestra."

  2. ★

    On October 16 and 17, 1891, the new Chicago Orchestra gave its first concerts at the Auditorium Theatre, with Thomas conducting.

  3. ★

    On December 14, 1904, Thomas led the dedication concert at Orchestra Hall on Michigan Avenue, the orchestra's own home, designed by Daniel Burnham.

  4. ★

    He had caught influenza during rehearsals. Thomas died on January 4, 1905, about three weeks after the hall opened.

  5. ★

    Frederick Stock, who had joined as a violist in 1895, led the orchestra for 37 years, from 1905 to 1942. In 1913 it took the name Chicago Symphony Orchestra.
