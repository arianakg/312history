BERTHA PALMER

A Chicago hostess bought Impressionist paintings when they were still considered radical. Today they hang in the Art Institute.

◉ Art Institute of Chicago (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1870 Bertha Honoré married Potter Palmer, the merchant who built State Street and the Palmer House.

  2. ★

    Advised by Sara Hallowell and Mary Cassatt, she bought Impressionist art when it was still seen as radical. Over the years she owned dozens of Monets.

  3. ★

    In 1893 she was president of the Board of Lady Managers at the World's Columbian Exposition, overseeing the Woman's Building.

  4. ★

    In 1910 she turned to Florida, buying large tracts of land around Sarasota and running ranches there.

  5. ★

    She died in 1918. In 1922 the Art Institute of Chicago received the Potter Palmer Collection, still the core of its Impressionist holdings.
