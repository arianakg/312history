CHICAGO BOARD OF TRADE BUILDING

At the end of LaSalle Street, an Art Deco tower is crowned by a faceless goddess of grain.

◉ LaSalle St (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The first Board of Trade Building on this site, designed by W. W. Boyington, opened on May 1, 1885. It was the tallest building in Chicago at the time.

  2. ★

    Its great trading room was the heart of the grain market. By the 1920s the building had become structurally unsound, and it was demolished in 1929.

  3. ★

    The new Art Deco building by Holabird & Root opened in 1930. Rising 44 stories, it was Chicago's tallest building on completion.

  4. ★

    On top stands Ceres, Roman goddess of grain: a roughly 31-foot aluminum statue by John Storrs, faceless and holding wheat and corn.

  5. ★

    It was named a Chicago Landmark on May 4, 1977, and a National Historic Landmark in 1978.
