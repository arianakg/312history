CHICAGO FIRSTS

Five Chicago firsts that changed everyday life.

◉ Chicago (map pin approximate)

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1885 the Home Insurance Building rose 10 stories in Chicago. It is often called the world's first skyscraper: a tall office building held up by a fireproof metal frame, not just thick walls.

  2. ★

    On June 6, 1892, Chicago's first "L" line opened. Small steam trains ran 3.6 miles from Congress Street to 39th Street in about 14 minutes. Much of that route is still part of the Green Line today.

  3. ★

    On Thanksgiving Day, November 28, 1895, the Chicago Times-Herald race became the first automobile race in the United States. In snow and slush, J. Frank Duryea's motor wagon finished the roughly 54-mile Chicago–Evanston round trip at about 7 miles per hour.

  4. ★

    On May 12, 1930, the Adler Planetarium opened to the public — the first planetarium in the Western Hemisphere. Chicago businessman Max Adler paid for the building, a Zeiss projector, and a collection of old astronomy instruments.

  5. ★

    On April 15, 1956, Chicago station WNBQ became the first full-time color television station in the United States. All of its local live programs were broadcast in color, from studios in the Merchandise Mart.
