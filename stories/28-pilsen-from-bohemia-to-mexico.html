PILSEN: FROM BOHEMIA TO MEXICO

One Chicago neighborhood, two homelands: first Bohemia, then Mexico.

◉ Pilsen (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    After the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, Bohemian (Czech) immigrants settled along 18th Street. A restaurant called "At the City of Plzen" honored Plzen in Bohemia, and people began calling the area Pilsen.

  2. ★

    St Procopius parish was organized in 1875 for Czech Catholics. Its big Romanesque church was dedicated in 1883 and still stands as a link to Bohemian Pilsen.

  3. ★

    In the 1950s and 1960s, many Mexican families moved to Pilsen. Some were pushed out of the Near West Side by expressways and the new University of Illinois campus. By 1970, Mexican-born residents were nearly half of Pilsen.

  4. ★

    From the 1960s and 1970s, murals, signs and bright colors brought Mexican culture to the old Bohemian buildings. In 2006 the Pilsen Historic District joined the National Register of Historic Places.

  5. ★

    In 1982, teacher Carlos Tortolero and other educators founded a Mexican art museum. It opened in Pilsen in 1987 and is now the National Museum of Mexican Art.
