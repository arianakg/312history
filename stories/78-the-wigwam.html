THE WIGWAM

In May 1860, a wooden hall in Chicago, built in weeks, made Abraham Lincoln a presidential nominee.

◉ The Wigwam, Lake St & Wacker (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1860, the young Republican Party picked Chicago for its national convention, set for May 16 to 18.

  2. ★

    Chicago backers paid for a two-story wooden hall at Lake and Market streets (today Lake and Wacker), started in April. People called it the Wigwam. Estimates of how many it held ran from about 6,000 to 15,000.

  3. ★

    On May 18, the first ballot put New York's William Seward ahead, 173½ votes to Lincoln's 102. A candidate needed 233.

  4. ★

    On the third ballot Lincoln reached 231½, just short. Ohio then moved four more votes to him, and he won the nomination.

  5. ★

    Lincoln was not in the hall. He waited at home in Springfield while friends, led by Judge David Davis, worked the delegates.
