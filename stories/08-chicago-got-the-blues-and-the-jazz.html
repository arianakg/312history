CHICAGO GOT THE BLUES, AND THE JAZZ

In 1922 a young cornet player from New Orleans stepped off the train in Chicago to join King Oliver's Creole Jazz Band: Louis Armstrong. From 1925 to 1928 he made his first records as a bandleader here for OKeh, with the Hot Five and Hot Seven: about sixty recordings that transformed jazz.

★ 2 FACTS

  1. ★

    Two decades later, in 1943, Muddy Waters came up from Mississippi. In 1948 his single "I Can't Be Satisfied" on Aristocrat Records became a hit. In 1950 brothers Leonard and Phil Chess renamed the label Chess Records, and Muddy recorded for them until 1975, landing 16 top-20 R&B hits, including Willie Dixon's "I'm Your Hoochie Coochie Man." Many call him the Father of Chicago Blues.

  2. ★

    From the South Side to the world: you can hear Chicago blues in the Rolling Stones, Led Zeppelin and beyond.
