THE FIRST ROTARY CLUB

Did you know the world's Rotary clubs started in Chicago?

◉ Room 711, Unity Building, 127 N. Dearborn Street, Chicago (The Loop) (map pin approximate)

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    The world's Rotary clubs started in Chicago

  2. ★

    On February 23, 1905, four men met downtown

  3. ★

    Lawyer Paul Harris called them together in Room 711 of the Unity Building, on Dearborn Street

  4. ★

    They named it Rotary because the meetings moved from office to office

  5. ★

    The Unity Building is gone. A rebuilt Room 711 stands in Evanston today

On February 23, 1905, four men met in Chicago to start what became the first Rotary club in the world. Paul P. Harris, a lawyer who had settled in Chicago in 1897, invited Gustavus Loehr, a mining engineer; Silvester Schiele, a coal dealer; and Hiram E. Shorey, a merchant tailor, to Loehr's office, Room 711 of the Unity Building, at 127 N. Dearborn Street (older directories listed the building as 75–81 Dearborn). Schiele became the first president of the Rotary Club of Chicago. They named the club Rotary because the meetings moved from one member's office to another.

A February 1925 article in The Rotarian put the founding in plain words: the first Rotary club in the world was formed in Chicago, February 23, 1905, by Paul Harris, a lawyer, who suggested the form of organization to three friends. Encyclopedia of Chicago records the same origin, and notes that Rotary International has had its headquarters in Evanston since the 1950s.

The Unity Building, designed by Clinton J. Warren and completed in 1892, was demolished in 1989. The site is now part of Block 37. Before the building came down, the furnishings of Room 711 were saved. A reconstruction of that room has been hosted at One Rotary Center, Rotary International's headquarters in Evanston, since 1994. It was moved to the first floor in 2014 and remains on official headquarters tours.

The map pin is the historic Unity Building address, 127 N. Dearborn Street, and is approximate. The photograph on this page is a portrait of Paul P. Harris, published in The Rotarian in February 1925, with a credit to Tom Phillips, Chicago. It shows Harris later in life, not at the 1905 meeting.
