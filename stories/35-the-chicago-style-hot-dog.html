THE CHICAGO-STYLE HOT DOG

Dragged through the garden: seven toppings, and a famous no-ketchup tradition.

◉ Chicago (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    A Chicago-style hot dog starts simple: an all-beef frank in a steamed poppy-seed bun. Then come the "Chicago Seven": yellow mustard, neon-green relish, chopped white onion, tomato, a dill pickle spear, sport peppers, and a dash of celery salt.

  2. ★

    The story goes back to the 1893 World's Fair. The Chicago History Museum tells how Emil Reichel and Samuel Ladany sold sausages with onions and mustard there, stayed in Chicago, and opened the store and factory that became Vienna Beef.

  3. ★

    The full loaded style came together in hard times. During the Great Depression, vendors at Maxwell Street and other stands piled cheap vegetables onto a five-cent frank, making a small sausage feel like a full meal.

  4. ★

    That pile of toppings earned a nickname: a hot dog "dragged through the garden." Mustard goes on first, celery salt goes on last, and every topping has its place along the bun.

  5. ★

    And ketchup? Leaving it off is a strong Chicago tradition — many stands will not put it on a Chicago dog, and some will not serve it at all. It is a tradition, not a law: the classic recipe simply has no ketchup in it.

PRIMARY SOURCES & CREDITS

Sources: Chicago History Museum, "Foods of the 1893 World's Fair" (Vienna Beef: Reichel and Ladany at the 1893 fair, then a Chicago store and factory; no-ketchup rule); NPR / WBEZ, "From Vienna Beef to PBR: Five Food and Drink Legacies of the 1893 World's Fair" (Depression-era toppings, cheap vegetables); PBS Chicago Stories, "A Symphony on a Bun: The Chicago-Style Hot Dog" (Chicago Seven toppings, steamed poppy-seed bun, dragged through the garden, no ketchup tradition). Hot-dog origins are debated, so the 1893 fair link is presented as the Chicago History Museum / Vienna Beef story, not as a proven single invention.
