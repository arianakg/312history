THE PIONEER ZEPHYR

Did you know a silver train raced from Denver to Chicago in one day?

◉ The Pioneer Zephyr, Museum of Science and Industry, 5700 S. Lake Shore Drive, Chicago (Hyde Park) (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 7 FACTS

  1. ★

    Did you know a silver train raced from Denver to Chicago in one day?

  2. ★

    On May 26, 1934, the Zephyr ran nonstop from Denver to Chicago

  3. ★

    The trip was 1,015 miles and took 13 hours and 5 minutes

  4. ★

    The Burlington, a Chicago railroad, had it built of stainless steel

  5. ★

    It ran on diesel power, not steam

  6. ★

    After the run, it was shown at Chicago's World's Fair

  7. ★

    The train still stands today at the Museum of Science and Industry

The train was built in 1934 by the Budd Company in Philadelphia for the Chicago, Burlington & Quincy, a Chicago railroad. It was first named the Burlington Zephyr and was renamed Pioneer Zephyr in 1936, the name used at the Museum of Science and Industry and in this story.

The Museum of Science and Industry and the Burlington railroad's own 1934 'Dawn-to-Dusk' card both give the same official figures used here: a nonstop run from Denver to Chicago on May 26, 1934, 1,015 miles, in 13 hours and 5 minutes (785 minutes). The card also lists an average of 77.6 miles an hour and a top speed of 112.5 miles an hour; those numbers are not used on the reel. Clock times on period sources disagree only by time-zone labeling, so the reel uses the duration only.

The Burlington card says the record was clocked when the train reached Halsted Street, Chicago. Regular service began November 11, 1934, on a Lincoln–Omaha–Kansas City route.

After the record run, the train was shown at Chicago's Century of Progress in 1934. It later appeared at the Chicago Railroad Fair in 1948–49. It was given to the Museum of Science and Industry (accession 60.14) and is still on display, and can be toured, at 5700 S. Lake Shore Drive, Chicago (Hyde Park).
