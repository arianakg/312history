CHICAGO BASKETBALL

Did you know the Harlem Globetrotters started in Chicago?

Basketball was invented in December 1891 by James Naismith, in Springfield, Massachusetts. By February 1893, Chicago-area YMCA teams had already formed a league.

The University of Chicago soon fielded a team. On January 18, 1896, Chicago played Iowa in the first intercollegiate five-man game — in Iowa City, not in Chicago. The team photo on this page is from the 1896 university yearbook, Cap and Gown.

In 1926, Abe Saperstein founded the Harlem Globetrotters in Chicago. Their first road game was on January 7, 1927, in Hinckley, Illinois. The name “Harlem” was a way to signal a Black team; they did not play a game in Harlem until 1968. They later played at the Savoy Ballroom on the South Side, which opened on November 23, 1927.

In 1966, the NBA awarded Chicago a new team, the Bulls, the city’s first lasting NBA franchise. The Bulls won six NBA titles in the 1990s. The Chicago Sky began play in the WNBA in 2006 and won the WNBA title in 2021. Both teams still play in Chicago today.
