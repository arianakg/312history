THE ATOMIC AGE, 1942

Under a football stadium in Chicago, the Atomic Age began quietly.

◉ University of Chicago (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    On December 2, 1942, under the west stands of Stagg Field at the University of Chicago, Enrico Fermi and his team made the first controlled, self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction.

  2. ★

    Their reactor, Chicago Pile-1 (CP-1), was a squash-court stack about 20 feet tall: 57 layers of graphite blocks holding uranium and uranium oxide, with cadmium rods to control it.

  3. ★

    Step by step on December 2, the rods were pulled out. The pile went critical that afternoon at just half a watt, ran for 28 minutes, then Fermi had it shut down.

  4. ★

    Arthur Compton reported success by phone in an improvised code: "The Italian navigator has landed in the New World." James Conant asked how the natives were. "Very friendly," Compton said.

  5. ★

    The work proved nuclear energy could be controlled. It led toward both nuclear power and nuclear weapons. The site became a National Historic Landmark in 1965, and Henry Moore's Nuclear Energy sculpture, unveiled there in 1967, marks the spot (mentioned only — not pictured).
