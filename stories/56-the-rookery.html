THE ROOKERY

Finished in 1888, this LaSalle Street landmark got its name from the birds that came before it.

◉ LaSalle St (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    After the Great Fire of 1871, a temporary City Hall and an old water tank stood on this corner. Birds roosted there, and people called the place "the rookery" — a name that stuck to the building that replaced them. Stories about the name vary.

  2. ★

    Burnham & Root were hired in 1885 to design a building on the site. The Rookery was completed in 1888 — twelve stories tall, one of the tallest buildings in the world at the time.

  3. ★

    It is a transitional skyscraper: heavy load-bearing masonry walls on the outside, combined with a skeletal iron frame inside. Root's lively ornament softens the powerful exterior.

  4. ★

    In 1905 Frank Lloyd Wright remodeled the skylit light court, covering much of the iron with gilded white marble and adding new light fixtures.

  5. ★

    The Rookery was named a Chicago Landmark on July 5, 1972, and a National Historic Landmark on May 15, 1975. A restoration completed in 1992 brought the light court back to its 1905 look.
