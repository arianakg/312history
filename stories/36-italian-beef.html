ITALIAN BEEF

Thin-sliced, juicy, and dipped: a Chicago working-class sandwich.

◉ Chicago (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    An Italian beef is slow-roasted beef, sliced very thin, piled onto a crusty Italian-style roll, and soaked with the beef's own cooking juices. Chicagoans call those juices "gravy," even though it is really a rich beef broth.

  2. ★

    Where did it start? The story goes back to Italian immigrant weddings. Culinary historians describe "peanut weddings," where cooks slow-cooked a tough, cheap cut of beef and sliced it thin, so one roast could feed a whole hall of guests on bread.

  3. ★

    No one inventor is proven. Al's says Al Ferreri, Frances Ferreri, and Chris Pacelli Sr. opened Al's #1 Italian Beef in Chicago in 1938 — but other families tell earlier stories, so 1938 is Al's claim, not a settled first.

  4. ★

    Ordering has its own language. "Dipped" or "wet" means the roll itself is dipped into the hot juices. "Dry" means just a little juice. The sandwich can be soaked all the way through — many people eat it leaning over the counter.

  5. ★

    "Hot" or "sweet"? Hot means hot giardiniera, the Chicago-style mix of pickled peppers and vegetables in oil. Sweet means roasted sweet peppers. Many regulars order both — hot and sweet together.
