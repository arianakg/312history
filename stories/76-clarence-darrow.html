CLARENCE DARROW

A lawyer from small-town Ohio moved to Chicago and took on trials the whole country followed.

◉ Woodlawn, 60th St (map pin approximate)

Follow on Instagram ★
Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    In 1887, at age 30, Clarence Darrow moved his family from Ohio to Chicago, where he built his law career.

  2. ★

    In 1894 he left his job as a railroad lawyer to defend union leader Eugene V. Debs after the Pullman Strike. He lost, but became one of the country's best-known labor lawyers.

  3. ★

    In 1907, in Boise, Idaho, he won an acquittal for labor leader Bill Haywood, who was accused of plotting the murder of a former Idaho governor.

  4. ★

    In 1924 he defended Nathan Leopold and Richard Loeb, two young men from wealthy Chicago families, for the murder of 14-year-old Bobby Franks. Both were spared the death penalty.

  5. ★

    In 1925 he defended teacher John Scopes in Tennessee's evolution trial. Darrow lived for years at 60th Street in Woodlawn, and after his death in 1938 his ashes were scattered in the Jackson Park lagoon.
