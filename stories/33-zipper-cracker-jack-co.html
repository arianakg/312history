ZIPPER, CRACKER JACK & CO

A zipper, a snack, a game and a gum all got their start in Chicago.

◉ Chicago (map pin approximate)

Watch the reel

★ 5 FACTS

  1. ★

    On August 29, 1893, Chicago inventor Whitcomb Judson patented his "clasp locker" for shoes, and showed it at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition through the Universal Fastener Company. It was an early, often-jamming step: the smoother modern zipper came later, improved by Gideon Sundback in 1913 — not in Chicago.

  2. ★

    In Chicago, brothers Frederick and Louis Rueckheim sold molasses-coated popcorn and peanuts. Louis found a way to keep the sweet pieces from sticking together, and in 1896 the brothers trademarked the name "Cracker Jack" and promoted it across the country.

  3. ★

    On Thanksgiving Day, November 24, 1887, at Chicago's Farragut Boat Club, George Hancock turned a tossed boxing glove and a broom handle into "indoor baseball," with a chalked diamond and written rules. The game later became softball, and Chicago still loves its big, barehanded 16-inch version.

  4. ★

    William Wrigley Jr. started his Chicago business in 1891, selling soap and then baking powder with free chewing gum. By 1892 the gum had won. He introduced Juicy Fruit in 1893, and Chicago became a gum and candy capital.

  5. ★

    Palmer House tells the story this way: for the 1893 fair, Bertha Palmer asked pastry chef Joseph Sehl for a portable chocolate dessert for ladies' lunch boxes. His walnut-topped, apricot-glazed chocolate cake is still sold as the Palmer House Brownie — though it was not called a "brownie" at the time.
