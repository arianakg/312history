Chicago's Grocery Gap

Chicago once had thousands of neighborhood grocery stores. By 1914, the city had about 7,400 groceries. After World War II, chains opened bigger stores in the suburbs, and in the 1960s and 1970s A&P, Kroger, and National Tea closed their smaller stores in the city. 11 of Chicago's 77 community areas had no SNAP-authorized supermarket, superstore, or large grocery store at the end of 2025. In 2013, that number was 5. The official count fell from 313 to 287. South of Madison Street, the count fell by 22. North of Madison, it fell by 4.

History: Encyclopedia of Chicago, "Grocery Stores and Supermarkets" and "National Tea Co."; City of Chicago, History of the Maxwell Street Market. Photos: Edwin Rosskam, South Side grocery, April 1941, Library of Congress, FSA/OWI (public domain); Curt Teich postcard, "Maxwell Street, Chicago," 1941, Newberry Library (public domain). Data: USDA FNS SNAP Retailer Locator Historical Data 2005–2025, current as of Dec. 31, 2025. Yearly snapshots are Dec. 31 counts of stores USDA classifies as a Supermarket, Super Store, or Large Grocery Store, mapped to official City of Chicago community-area polygons. North/south is measured against the official Madison Street centerline. These are not corner stores or shops that do not take SNAP. Report: Chicago Food Equity Council 2023 Annual Report. Solutions cited: City of Chicago Market City plan and the mayor's Oct. 22, 2026 executive order.
