1833 to today

Timeline

Frontier Town · 1803–1860

  • 1803: Fort Dearborn is built at the mouth of the Chicago River.
  • 1833: Chicago is incorporated as a town of about 350 people.
  • 1837: Chicago becomes a city; William B. Ogden is its first mayor.
  • 1848: The Illinois & Michigan Canal opens and the first railroad arrives.

Fire & Rebirth · 1860–1893

  • 1865: The Union Stock Yards open.
  • 1871: The Great Chicago Fire burns about 3.3 square miles and leaves some 100,000 homeless.
  • 1886: The Haymarket affair, a turning point for the labor movement.
  • 1889: Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr open Hull House.
  • 1893: The World's Columbian Exposition draws more than 27 million visits.

Big Shoulders · 1900–1945

  • 1900: The Chicago River's flow is reversed.
  • 1909: Burnham and Bennett publish the Plan of Chicago.
  • 1914: Weeghman Park, today's Wrigley Field, opens.
  • 1915: The Eastland capsizes in the river, killing 844 people.
  • 1929: The St. Valentine's Day Massacre.
  • 1942: The first controlled nuclear chain reaction, at the University of Chicago.

Modern Metropolis · 1945–2000

  • 1955: Richard J. Daley becomes mayor and serves 21 years.
  • 1958: O'Hare opens to commercial flights; it soon becomes the world's busiest airport.
  • 1973: Sears Tower is completed, the world's tallest building for 25 years.
  • 1983: Harold Washington becomes Chicago's first Black mayor.

Today · 2000–now

  • 2004: Millennium Park opens.
  • 2016: The Chicago Cubs win their first World Series since 1908.
  • 2019: Lori Lightfoot becomes the first Black woman to serve as mayor.
Behind the page

About

312history is an Instagram project by Ariana Cholponkadyrova, a Kyrgyz creator living in Chicago. It tells the city's story, one place, one date and one person at a time, from the frontier town of 1833 to the city of today.
